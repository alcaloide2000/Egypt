The Temple of Hathor at Dendera

One of the best-preserved temples in Egypt, still with its roof, its crypts and its staircases, built for Hathor, goddess of love, music and joy. It was begun under Cleopatra's father and finished by Roman emperors: inside, a forest of Hathor-faced columns holds up a ceiling painted with the sky, and on the back wall Cleopatra herself appears with her son by Julius Caesar.

Around and inside the temple

Twelve stops: the precinct first, then into the temple, down to the crypts, up to the roof and round to the back wall. Tap a number on the plan.

Simplified plan, not to scale; positions are approximate.

  1. 1

    The gate

    A monumental stone gateway of the Roman emperors Domitian and Trajan opens in the great mud-brick wall around the sacred precinct. From here the temple's façade fills the view.

  2. 2

    The birth houses

    Two small temples where the birth of Ihy, the child of Hathor and Horus, was celebrated each year: a Roman one, decorated under Trajan, with figures of the dwarf god Bes, protector of mothers and babies, and an older one begun by Nectanebo I around 370 BC.

    Bes ↗ · Nectanebo I ↗

  3. 3

    The Christian church

    Between the two birth houses stand the walls of a 5th-century basilica, one of the oldest churches in Egypt, built with blocks from the older birth house when the temples had closed.

  4. 4

    The sanatorium

    Mud-brick rooms where the sick came to sleep and bathe in water that had run over magic texts, hoping for a healing dream from Hathor: an early hospital of faith.

  5. 5

    The pronaos and the Hathor columns

    The great columned hall at the front, built under the emperor Tiberius: 24 huge columns whose capitals are the face of Hathor, with cow's ears, on four sides, crowned with a little shrine like her sacred rattle, the sistrum. Many faces were hacked off by Christians.

    Hathor ↗

  6. 6

    The astronomical ceiling

    Look up: the ceiling of the pronaos is a map of the sky, with the long body of the goddess Nut swallowing and giving birth to the sun, the hours of day and night, the decans and the signs of the zodiac. Cleaned of centuries of soot in the 2000s and 2010s, its blues and golds shine again.

    Nut ↗ · The zodiac ↓

  7. 7

    The Hall of Appearances

    A smaller hall of six columns where the goddess's statue appeared at festivals. Side rooms held the temple's treasures: one was the laboratory, where recipes for perfumes and sacred oils are carved on the walls.

  8. 8

    The sanctuary

    At the back, the dark sanctuary held Hathor's sacred boat, surrounded by a corridor of chapels for the gods who shared the temple. The rooms grow smaller and darker as you go in.

  9. 9

    The crypts

    Narrow chambers hidden in the thickness of the walls and under the floor stored the temple's most precious statues. One is carved with a great lotus giving birth to a snake inside a long bubble: a creation myth, not, as some claim, an ancient light bulb.

  10. 10

    The staircases

    Two staircases climb to the roof, carved with priests carrying the goddess's statue in procession. At New Year it was taken up to a small kiosk on the roof to be united with the rays of the rising sun, and brought down again by the other stair.

  11. 11

    The roof and the zodiac

    On the roof, small chapels for the mysteries of Osiris. The ceiling of one held the famous circular zodiac, cut out in 1821 and taken to Paris; it is in the Louvre, and a cast stands in its place. The view over the fields to the Nile is wonderful.

    Osiris ↗ · The zodiac ↓

  12. 12

    Cleopatra on the back wall

    Walk round to the outside of the back wall: Cleopatra VII and her son by Julius Caesar, Caesarion, offer to the gods, carved larger than life. It is one of the very few large images of the famous queen.

    Cleopatra VII ↗ · Caesarion ↗

The Dendera zodiac

The only complete circular map of the sky to survive from ancient Egypt.

Schematic of the zodiac, not to scale; the signs are placed evenly, not as carved.

A Babylonian sky in Egypt

The twelve signs of the zodiac came to Egypt from Babylon through the Greeks; here they mix with Egyptian star figures like the hippopotamus and the bull's leg (the Great Bear) and the 36 decans, the star groups that told the hours of the night.

Held up by goddesses

The round disc of sky is held up by four standing goddesses and eight falcon-headed spirits, a way of drawing the heavens as a dome resting on supports.

A date in the stars

Two eclipses are shown on it; astronomers have matched them to 52 and 51 BC, so the zodiac was carved in the time of Cleopatra.

Taken to Paris

In 1821 the Frenchman Lelorrain cut it out with saws and gunpowder and shipped it to France. It is in the Louvre; the plaster cast on the roof shows where it was.

Hathor, the golden one

The golden one

Hathor was the goddess of love, music, dance, joy and drunkenness, the 'Golden One'. She was shown as a woman with cow's horns and a sun disk, as a cow, or as a face with cow's ears, as on the columns here.

Eye of Ra

In one myth the sun god sent her as his fierce Eye to punish rebellious humans; she was calmed only with beer dyed red to look like blood. Her festivals included a feast of drunkenness to remember it.

The Beautiful Meeting

Every summer Hathor's statue sailed upriver from Dendera to Edfu to visit her husband Horus, a festival of two weeks. Their child was Ihy, the young musician god celebrated in the birth houses.

Music for the goddess

The sistrum, a sacred rattle, and the menat necklace were her instruments. The capitals of the columns imitate the sistrum, so the whole pronaos is a hall of music in stone.

Dendera through time

  1. Old and New Kingdom

    Earlier temples to Hathor stand here; later inscriptions claim plans going back to the time of the pyramid builders.

  2. c. 370 BC

    Nectanebo I builds a birth house.

  3. 54 BC

    Under Ptolemy XII, Cleopatra's father, the present temple is begun.

  4. c. 50–30 BC

    Cleopatra VII and Caesarion are carved on the back wall; the zodiac is made around 50 BC.

  5. AD 14–37

    Under the emperor Tiberius the pronaos with its Hathor columns is built.

  6. c. AD 100

    The Roman birth house is decorated under Trajan; Domitian and Trajan build the gate.

  7. 5th century

    A Christian basilica is built between the birth houses; faces in the temple are hacked out.

  8. 1798–1799

    Napoleon's scholars draw the temple and the zodiac.

  9. 1821

    The circular zodiac is removed to Paris.

  10. 2000s–2010s

    The pronaos ceiling is cleaned and its colours reappear.

For our visit

Look up first

In the pronaos, stand still and look at the ceiling before anything else; a small torch or phone light helps in the darker halls.

Go up to the roof

Climb one staircase and come down the other, as the priests did; the roof chapels and the view are worth it.

Ask for the crypt

The crypt with the lotus-and-snake relief is reached by a low passage; ask the guardian which one is open.

Walk round the back

Do not leave without seeing Cleopatra and Caesarion on the outer back wall, and the lion-headed water spouts on the sides.
