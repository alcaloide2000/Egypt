The Grand Egyptian Museum

The largest museum in the world given to a single civilisation, beside the pyramids of Giza: a colossal Ramesses II in the hall, a staircase of giant statues climbing towards a window on the pyramids, galleries that walk through three thousand years, and, for the first time, the whole treasure of Tutankhamun in one place.

Through the museum

Ten stops, from the obelisk outside, through the hall and up the staircase, then the main galleries, Tutankhamun and the boat. Tap a number on the plan.

Schematic plan, not to scale; the real layout is more complex, so follow the museum's signs.

  1. 1

    The hanging obelisk

    Outside the entrance, a granite obelisk of Ramesses II stands raised on supports, so you can walk underneath and see the king's cartouche carved on its base, a part no one was ever meant to see. It came from Tanis in the Delta.

  2. 2

    Ramesses II in the Grand Atrium

    The colossal red granite statue of Ramesses II, about 11 m tall and some 83 tonnes, greets you in the entrance hall. It stood for decades in a Cairo square, was moved to Giza in 2006 and placed here in 2018.

    Ramesses II card ↗

  3. 3

    The Grand Staircase

    A six-storey staircase lined with dozens of colossal statues, sarcophagi and architectural pieces, arranged by themes: the image of the king, the houses of the gods, the service of the gods and the journey to the afterlife. Take it slowly on the way up.

  4. 4

    The view of the pyramids

    At the top of the staircase, a huge window frames the pyramids of Giza, about 2 km away: the museum was laid out on a line towards them.

    Giza guide ↗

  5. 5

    Galleries: prehistory to the Old Kingdom

    The main galleries run in time order. The first part covers the earliest Egyptians, the first kings and the age of the pyramids: statues of Old Kingdom kings and officials, tomb goods and everyday objects.

  6. 6

    Galleries: Middle and New Kingdom

    Royal statues of the Middle Kingdom, then the empire of the New Kingdom: Hatshepsut, Thutmose III, Amenhotep III, Akhenaten and the Ramessides, with objects from temples and tombs across Egypt.

  7. 7

    Galleries: Late Period to Roman Egypt

    The last centuries of pharaonic Egypt, the Ptolemies and Roman Egypt: Greek-style portraits, mummy portraits and the meeting of Egyptian and Greek gods you saw in Alexandria.

  8. 8

    The Tutankhamun galleries

    For the first time the whole contents of his tomb, more than 5,000 objects, are shown together, in the order they were found by Howard Carter in 1922. Allow at least an hour.

    Highlights ↓ · Valley of the Kings guide ↗

  9. 9

    The Khufu boat museum

    In its own building: Khufu's cedar boat, 43 m long, found in 1954 beside the Great Pyramid and moved here in 2021. Its twin, the second boat, is being rebuilt from its pieces, sometimes in view of visitors.

    The boat ↓

  10. 10

    Gardens and the building

    The building, by the Irish architects Heneghan Peng, has a vast translucent stone facade patterned with triangles, a nod to the pyramids. Its gardens and terraces have cafés and shade.

Tutankhamun: eight things to find

His tomb, KV62 in the Valley of the Kings, was found almost intact by Howard Carter on 4 November 1922.

  1. 1

    The gold mask

    Over 10 kg of gold inlaid with lapis lazuli, carnelian and glass, placed directly over the mummy's head and shoulders. The vulture and cobra on the brow are Nekhbet and Wadjet, the two goddesses of the crowns.

  2. 2

    The innermost coffin

    Of solid gold, about 110 kg, the innermost of three nested coffins inside a stone sarcophagus. The king holds the crook and flail of Osiris.

  3. 3

    The gilded shrines

    Four great shrines of gilded wood, fitted one inside another like boxes, filled the burial chamber almost wall to wall around the sarcophagus.

  4. 4

    The golden throne

    The back shows Tutankhamun and his wife Ankhesenamun under the rays of the Aten, the sun disk of Akhenaten's religion: made before the young king returned Egypt to Amun.

  5. 5

    The chariots

    Six chariots, taken apart to fit into the tomb, some for display and some for hunting or war, with their gilded and inlaid bodies.

  6. 6

    The meteorite dagger

    One of two daggers wrapped with the mummy has a blade of iron from a meteorite, at a time when iron was rarer than gold.

  7. 7

    The canopic shrine

    A gilded shrine guarded by four goddesses with outstretched arms, Isis, Nephthys, Neith and Selket, held the alabaster chest with the king's embalmed organs.

  8. 8

    Everyday things

    Sandals, gloves, linen, board games, beds, boxes of food and wine jars: the belongings of a young man who died at about nineteen, around 1323 BC.

Khufu's boat

Found in a sealed pit

In 1954 Kamal el-Mallakh opened a pit beside the Great Pyramid and found a cedar boat taken apart into more than 1,200 pieces, made for Khufu around 2560 BC.

Rebuilt without a plan

The restorer Hag Ahmed Youssef Moustafa spent more than a decade putting it back together, guided only by the builders' marks on the wood.

Moved in one piece

In August 2021 the 43 m boat travelled from the pyramid to the museum in a climate-controlled cradle on a remote-controlled vehicle.

For our visit

Tutankhamun first or last?

The Tutankhamun galleries are the busiest. If the queue is short when you arrive, go there first and see the rest after.

Pace yourself

The museum is enormous and the walks are long. After a morning at Saqqara and Giza, choose the four musts and leave the rest.

Look for the pyramids

Stop at the top of the staircase and at the windows of the galleries: the pyramids appear again and again in the views.

Photos

Photography without flash is usually allowed in the galleries; check the signs, as some rooms may have their own rules.
