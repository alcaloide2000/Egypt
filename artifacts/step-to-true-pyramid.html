Old Kingdom · c. 2670–2590 BC

From Step to True Pyramid

In about eighty years, Egypt's builders went from Djoser's stairway of stone at Saqqara to Sneferu's smooth-sided Red Pyramid at Dahshur. Each of these four pyramids fixed a problem with the one before it. All drawings use the same scale, from the real measurements.

The angle problem

The same four outlines placed on top of each other. Meidum and Khufu share the steep angle of about 52°; the Bent Pyramid starts even steeper and backs off to the Red Pyramid's safer 43.4°.

1Saqqara · Dynasty 3 · c. 2670–2650 BC

Step Pyramid of Djoser

Height
62.5 m
Base
121 × 109 m
Form
six steps

Djoser's architect Imhotep built Egypt's first monument entirely in stone. It began as a square mastaba, a flat-topped tomb, and grew in stages to six steps, each one a smaller mastaba set on the one below. The stones lean inward in layers, which keeps the stack stable.

The idea: stack mastabas into a stairway to the sky.

2Meidum · end of Dynasty 3 to Dynasty 4 · c. 2600 BC

Pyramid of Meidum

Planned
c. 92 m, base 144 m
Today
c. 65 m tower
Angle
c. 52°

Probably begun for Huni as a step pyramid of seven, then eight, steps. Sneferu later filled in the steps and added a smooth outer casing, making the first attempt at a true pyramid. The casing rested on sand rather than rock and was not bonded to the core. It later fell away, leaving the stepped tower standing on its own rubble. When it collapsed is still debated.

The lesson: a smooth skin needs a solid footing and a bonded core.

3Dahshur · Sneferu, Dynasty 4 · c. 2600 BC

Bent Pyramid

Height
101 m
Base
188.6 m
Angle
54.5°, then 43.4°

Designed as a true pyramid from the start, at a steep 54.5°. The soft clay ground below began to settle and the chambers cracked, so the builders changed to a gentler 43.4° at about 47 m, which gave the pyramid its bend. Much of its fine white limestone casing still survives.

The fix: lower the angle and reduce the weight.

4Dahshur · Sneferu, Dynasty 4 · c. 2590s BC

Red Pyramid

Height
104 m
Base
220 m
Angle
43.4°

Sneferu's third pyramid used the safer 43.4° from the base up and stands on firmer ground. It worked: the first successful smooth-sided pyramid, named for the reddish limestone of its core, now that its white casing is gone. It was probably Sneferu's tomb. A generation later his son Khufu built the Great Pyramid at Giza at a steeper 51.8°, on solid rock.

The result: the first true pyramid, and the model for Giza.
